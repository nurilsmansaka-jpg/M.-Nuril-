Although February 8th is not an official holiday in the United States, Super Bowl Sunday has evolved into a massive cultural phenomenon that feels like one, drawing tens of millions of viewers worldwide to watch the NFL’s championship game. Since its humble beginnings in 1967, when the Green Bay Packers played in a partially empty stadium, the event has transformed into a global spectacle that transcends sports; today, because stadium tickets cost thousands of dollars, most Americans celebrate by hosting large home parties filled with iconic foods like pizza and wings, making it one of the highest food-consumption days of the year. Beyond the football field, the Super Bowl is equally famous for its entertainment, featuring the national anthem and the legendary Halftime Show, where world-class performers like Beyoncé and Kendrick Lamar have turned the game into one of the biggest musical stages in the world.
